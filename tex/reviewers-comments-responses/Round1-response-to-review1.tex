\refereeOverallComment{2}{
    The manuscript presents axionbloch, an open-source Python package with a C++ backend (via pybind11). It numerically integrates the Bloch equations for spin-1/2 ensembles subject to real magnetic fields and to the pseudomagnetic field produced by the axion-nucleon gradient coupling. The package uses astropy for unit handling, integrates in a rotating coordinate frame with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme, and models bias-field inhomogeneity by sampling an ensemble of bias fields.

    The author benchmarks the code against standard NMR behaviour: free induction decay and spin echoes, continuous-wave excitation, and $T_1$ relaxation of a hyperpolarised sample. Agreement is quantified by a normalised residual metric, with discrepancies below $10^{-4}$. The manuscript then demonstrates an axion simulation for a hyperpolarised liquid $^{129}$Xe sample in the standard halo model, showing single-realisation and ensemble-averaged (1000 realisations) transverse magnetisation, and reports a runtime of 2.7 s for $7\times 10^8$ integration steps. The code, documentation and test suite are publicly available.

    The manuscript is clearly written and describes a well-structured, usable piece of software. The architecture is sensible, the benchmarks behave as expected, and the open-source, tested and documented delivery is commendable. I expect the package will be useful to groups designing and analysing spin-based axion searches, and for teaching, as the author notes.

    That said, the manuscript does not contain new physical results. The physics implemented is well established: the Bloch equations, the axion-gradient pseudomagnetic field, and the stochastic description of the virialised halo field. So are the numerical methods (fixed-step RK4 in a rotating frame). The calibration results are correct but predictable, since they reproduce textbook NMR behaviour. The axion simulation reproduces qualitative signal features already described analytically in the literature. The contribution is therefore a software and methods paper.

}

I am very glad to see the positive evaluation and constructive comments from the referee. 
Point-to-point responses are addressed as follows.

\refereeNoTwoBox{2}{
    Are there shape anisotropy effects that must be taken into account with regards to the magnetisation dynamics of the sample? Is the shape of the sample important e.g. spherical vs cylindrical?
}

\response{2}{
Yes, and the effect can be taken into account by settings in the \texttt{axionbloch} simulation.
Shape anisotropy effects can influence the magnetization dynamics of the sample. 
The shape of the sample can affect the distribution of the magnetic field across the NMR sample and, consequently, the spin dynamics. 
For instance, a spherical sample will experience a different magnetic field distribution compared to a cylindrical one. 
Therefore, it is important to consider these effects when doing simulations.

In \texttt{axionbloch}, users can account for a specified field distribution by setting the field spread and relative sampling weights through \texttt{Magnet.B\_spread} and \texttt{Magnet.ratios}. 
This allows users to model the effective field inhomogeneity associated with a particular sample geometry.
}
